Wound documentation & monitoring

A consistent record.
A view over time.

Consistent documentation can help the care team maintain a clearer record of how a wound is changing throughout treatment.

Where supported by Wound Haven’s clinical systems, documentation may include:

  • Wound measurements
  • Wound characteristics
  • Clinical photographs
  • Treatment history
  • Progress notes
  • Care-plan updates
  • Ongoing monitoring

Tracking changes over time gives clinicians additional context when evaluating how a wound is responding to care.

Clinical insight

Additional information.
Clinical context.

Some chronic or complex wounds may require additional diagnostic information to better understand factors affecting the wound. Depending on the patient’s clinical needs, diagnostic support may include:

Wound Cultures

Cultures may help identify organisms present in a wound and support treatment decisions.

Laboratory Support

Labs may provide additional clinical information relevant to the patient’s condition.

Clinically Indicated Imaging

Imaging may be incorporated when clinically indicated.

Diagnostic support depends on clinical need and current capabilities.

Mobile wound care

Tools that support care.
Where the patient is.

Wound Haven’s care model brings wound-care services to appropriate patients in the environments where they live or receive care. Technology can support clinical workflows in:

Patient Homes

Documentation and assessment at the bedside.

Skilled Nursing Facilities

Clinical information and wound-care coordination with facility teams.

Assisted Living

Mobile care delivered within the resident’s care environment.

Rehabilitation Facilities

Wound-care documentation and coordination during rehabilitation.

LTAC Settings

Support for appropriate patients with more complex care needs.

What technology may Wound Haven use?

Wound Haven may use technology to support wound assessment, documentation, monitoring, diagnostics, treatment, and care coordination. Specific technologies should be confirmed based on current clinical capabilities.

Does technology replace clinical judgment?

No. Technology supports the clinical process. Assessment and treatment decisions remain based on the patient’s wound and clinical needs.

How can changes in a wound be monitored?

Clinical documentation and ongoing assessment may be used to monitor how a wound changes throughout care.

Explore Documentation & Monitoring ↑
Can wound imaging or clinical photographs be used?

Imaging or photographic documentation may be used where supported by Wound Haven’s current clinical systems and when clinically appropriate.

Can vascular assessment be part of wound evaluation?

Vascular assessment, including ABI or Doppler where clinically appropriate, may be part of wound evaluation.

Explore Vascular Assessment →
Can negative pressure wound therapy be part of care?

Negative pressure wound therapy may be used for appropriate patients and wounds as part of an individualized treatment plan.

Explore NPWT →
Does Wound Haven serve Austin?

Wound Haven serves Austin, Houston, San Antonio, College Station, Corpus Christi, Beaumont, and surrounding areas.

Is care provided in an office?

Wound Haven provides mobile wound care in patient homes and supported facility environments rather than through a traditional office-based model.
