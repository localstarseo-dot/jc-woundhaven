Wound assessment

Understand the wound.
Plan the next step.

A comprehensive wound assessment helps establish a clearer picture of the patient’s wound, clinical needs, and care environment.

Depending on the patient’s situation, assessment may consider:

  • Wound location and characteristics
  • Wound progression
  • Signs of infection or complication
  • Circulation concerns
  • Swelling
  • Patient health factors
  • Current treatment
  • Home or facility care environment

Treatment & management

Care guided by
the clinical situation.

Treatment depends on the type of wound, the patient’s condition, and how the wound is progressing. Wound Haven’s clinical capabilities may include:

01

Wound Debridement

Removal of nonviable tissue when clinically indicated.

02

Wound Dressing Management

Selection and management of appropriate wound dressings based on the care plan.

03

Compression Therapy

Compression therapy for appropriate venous, swelling, or lymphedema-related conditions.

04

Negative Pressure Wound Therapy

Use and management of wound-vac or NPWT therapy for appropriate wounds.

05

Patient & Caregiver Education

Clear guidance to help patients and caregivers understand the wound-care plan.

Clinical care may also consider factors that could affect the patient’s ability to heal.

Diagnostic & clinical support

Clinical information.
More informed decisions.

Some wounds require additional clinical information to understand why healing may be delayed or what treatment may be appropriate. Where clinically indicated, care may involve:

Vascular Assessment

Including ABI or Doppler assessment where appropriate.

Wound Cultures

Cultures may help identify microorganisms and support treatment decisions.

Laboratory Testing

Laboratory testing may provide additional information about the patient’s health.

Imaging

Imaging may be incorporated when clinically appropriate.

Guided Antibiotic Care

Culture and clinical findings may help guide antibiotic treatment when medically appropriate.

Biopsy

Biopsy may be considered when additional diagnostic evaluation is needed.

Advanced wound care

More options for
chronic or complex wounds.

Some chronic or complex wounds may require additional treatment approaches beyond standard wound management.

Depending on the patient’s wound and clinical needs, options may include:

  • Negative Pressure Wound Therapy
  • Skin Graft Substitutes
  • Placental Membrane Products
  • Advanced Dressings
  • Compression Therapy
  • Ongoing Wound Monitoring
  • Coordinated Clinical Follow-Up

What wound-care services does Wound Haven provide?

Wound Haven’s clinical capabilities include wound assessment, debridement, compression therapy, vascular assessment, diagnostic support, negative pressure wound therapy, advanced wound-care options, education, and ongoing wound management where clinically appropriate.

Does Wound Haven provide mobile wound care?

Yes. Wound Haven provides mobile and bedside wound-care services for appropriate patients in homes and supported facility environments.

Do patients need to visit an office?

Wound Haven does not currently operate an in-office care model. Care is provided where appropriate patients live or receive care.

What types of wounds may need these services?

Wound Haven may support patients with diabetic wounds, pressure injuries, venous and arterial wounds, lymphedema-related wounds, burns, skin conditions, non-healing wounds, and other complex wounds.

Explore Wounds We Treat →
Does Wound Haven offer wound debridement?

Debridement is among Wound Haven’s clinical capabilities and may be used when clinically appropriate.

Can wound-vac or NPWT therapy be part of care?

Negative pressure wound therapy may be incorporated into care for appropriate patients and wounds.

How can vascular assessment help?

Vascular assessment, including ABI or Doppler where clinically appropriate, may help identify circulation factors affecting wound healing.

Are wound-care services available in Austin?

Austin is one of Wound Haven’s primary service areas, along with Houston and San Antonio.
