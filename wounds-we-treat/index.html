Diabetic wound care

Diabetic wounds.
Individual care needs.

Diabetes can affect circulation, sensation, and the body’s ability to heal. Wounds in patients with diabetes may require careful assessment, ongoing monitoring, and treatment based on the wound and the patient’s overall clinical needs.

Wound Haven may support diabetic wound care through services such as:

  • Wound assessment
  • Vascular assessment
  • Debridement when appropriate
  • Dressing management
  • Infection-related evaluation
  • Ongoing wound monitoring
  • Patient and caregiver education

02

Pressure wound care

Pressure injuries.
Care shaped by clinical needs.

Pressure injuries can develop when prolonged pressure affects the skin and underlying tissue, particularly in patients with limited mobility.

Request a Wound Evaluation

Care may involve:

  • Wound assessment
  • Pressure-related risk evaluation
  • Debridement where appropriate
  • Dressing management
  • Ongoing monitoring
  • Education for patients, caregivers, or facility teams
  • Coordination with the patient’s broader care plan
03

Venous wound care

Venous wounds.
Swelling and circulation concerns.

Venous wounds may be associated with impaired blood return, swelling, and changes in the lower legs.

Learn About Compression Therapy and Vascular Assessment →

Depending on the patient’s clinical situation, wound care may include:

  • Wound evaluation
  • Vascular assessment
  • Compression therapy where appropriate
  • Dressing management
  • Monitoring for changes in the wound
  • Education and ongoing care support
Explore Compression Therapy →
04

Arterial wound care

Arterial wounds.
Careful clinical assessment.

Arterial wounds may occur when reduced blood flow affects the tissues and limits healing.

Where clinically appropriate, vascular assessment may include ABI or Doppler testing.

Learn About Vascular Assessment →

Assessment may include evaluation of:

  • Wound characteristics
  • Circulation concerns
  • Pain or tissue changes
  • Vascular status
  • Other factors that may affect healing

Lymphedema can contribute to swelling, skin changes, and wounds that may require ongoing wound management.

Burns & skin conditions

Burns and skin-related wounds.
Assessment comes first.

Wound Haven may provide assessment and wound-care support for appropriate patients based on the nature of the wound and the patient’s clinical condition.

Potential care may include:

  • Wound evaluation
  • Dressing management

  • Education
  • Coordination with other providers when needed

Chronic & non-healing wound care

Non-healing wounds.
When a wound isn’t progressing.

A wound that is not progressing may be affected by more than one factor.

Wound Haven evaluates the wound and surrounding clinical factors to help determine an appropriate next step.

Request a Wound Evaluation

Possible considerations can include:

  • Circulation
  • Infection
  • Diabetes
  • Pressure
  • Swelling
  • Nutrition
  • Mobility
  • Underlying health conditions
  • Current wound-care approach

When to contact Wound Haven

A wound concern?
Start with an evaluation.

Consider contacting Wound Haven when a wound:

Request a Wound Evaluation
  • Is not improving as expected
  • Continues to reopen
  • Has been present for an extended period
  • Is associated with diabetes
  • Is related to pressure or limited mobility
  • Is accompanied by significant swelling
  • May involve circulation concerns
  • Requires ongoing wound-care support
  • Needs assessment beyond routine dressing changes
Emergency medical care

Wound Haven does not replace emergency medical care. Seek emergency care for urgent or life-threatening symptoms.

What types of wounds does Wound Haven treat?

Wound Haven supports care for diabetic wounds, pressure injuries, venous wounds, arterial wounds, lymphedema-related wounds, burns and skin-related wounds, non-healing wounds, and other complex wounds.

Does Wound Haven care for chronic and non-healing wounds?

Yes. Chronic and non-healing wounds are part of Wound Haven’s wound-care focus, depending on the patient’s condition and clinical needs.

Does Wound Haven support diabetic wound care?

Yes. Wound Haven supports wound-care evaluation and treatment for patients with diabetic wounds, including related assessment and ongoing wound management.

Does Wound Haven treat pressure injuries?

Yes. Pressure injuries are among the wound categories Wound Haven supports.

What care may be used for venous wounds?

Wound Haven provides care for venous wounds and may incorporate services such as vascular assessment and compression therapy where clinically appropriate.

Explore Compression Therapy →
How are arterial wounds assessed?

Arterial wounds may require careful vascular assessment and clinical review. Wound Haven can evaluate appropriate patients and determine next steps.

Learn About Vascular Assessment →
What is a non-healing wound?

A non-healing wound is generally a wound that is not progressing as expected and may require additional clinical assessment to identify factors affecting healing.

Can wound care be provided at home or in a facility?

Yes. Wound Haven provides mobile and bedside wound-care services for appropriate patients in homes and supported care facilities.

Is wound care available in Austin?

Austin is one of Wound Haven’s primary service areas, along with Houston and San Antonio.
